## Agreement clusters

**The idea is not the problem - nine of eleven seats said so.** This is unusually
strong convergence, and it is worth naming precisely because the overall result
below is REVISE. `founder-wants-weirder` (REACTION: "somebody understood what we
are"), `brand-manager` (REACTION: "the first agency concept that has treated our
existing packaging as an asset"), `cmo-defender` (LANDS: the sole-proof line),
and even `builds-it-in-house` (LANDS: "the callout system. It is a good idea and
it is the right idea") independently praised the same concept. Four seats that
share almost nothing else agreed on this.

**The packaging callout system is the most-cited element in the run, and it is a
paragraph in the deck.** Cited approvingly by `brand-manager` (LANDS),
`performance-lead` (LANDS: "a repeatable message unit"), `builds-it-in-house`
(LANDS), and `cmo-defender` (CHANGE: "lead with the packaging callouts"). Four
seats, from four incompatible positions, pointed at the same secondary element.

**The films are the most-resisted element, and they are the headline.** Resisted
by `cmo-defender` (RESISTS: "the most expensive and slowest thing in the deck
and it is first"), `performance-lead` (RESISTS: "four long-form films as the
primary output... that is a content project"), `founder-wants-weirder` (RESISTS:
"documentary about a food company is a well-worn genre"), and
`agency-fatigued-vp` (capacity). Note the range: the founder thinks they are too
safe, the performance lead thinks they are too few, the CMO thinks they are too
slow. Different objections, same element.

**The commercial structure is unfinished, and both money seats said so
independently.** `procurement-lead` (MISSING: deliverable schedule, named team,
change-order trigger, notice period, film fee basis) and `finance-partner`
(MISSING: phasing, leading indicator, review gate).

## Consequential dissent

**`legal-reviewer` FLAG - the highest-consequence finding in the run.** The
central line of the pitch, "Marra is the only one who can prove it," is a
sole-superiority claim against an unnamed category. Alicia's CHANGE field
supplies the fix in one sentence: make it a claim about Marra rather than about
everyone else. "We never stopped doing it the long way" carries the identical
argument with zero comparative substantiation burden.

This is not a wording preference. If that line reaches packaging or paid media
as written, it needs current substantiation covering every competitor in the
category. This gate caps the score below.

**`procurement-lead` NO - the objection most likely to actually cost the
pitch.** Marcus has no view on the idea and can still stop the award. His
verdict is entirely structural and entirely fixable: a one-page deliverable
schedule and a fixed or explained film fee. He said so himself ("I would clear
this in a day with that"). Note that this is exactly the objection the refusal
gate surfaced before the run - the deck as presented carried no numbers at all.

**`finance-partner` NO reads as scope compression, not rejection.** Gerald's
CHANGE is explicit: phase it, two quarters, a leading indicator, a review gate,
and "I would say yes to that this week." Read this as the pitch heading toward a
smaller award rather than no award. That is a real outcome and Fieldhouse should
decide in advance whether a six-month phased version is acceptable.

**`builds-it-in-house` NO - the reusable one.** Ellery's objection is not to the
work, it is to the retainer swallowing creative wholesale when the genuine gap
is film production and outside perspective. Her CHANGE names the version she
would advocate FOR: scope to what the internal team cannot do, and put ongoing
creative through them with Fieldhouse art-directing.

**This is a hard-no describing the terms of her own conversion, which is the most
valuable thing this seat produces.** She did not convert - she stayed NO on the
pitch as written, correctly. But she named the structural gap.

**`agency-fatigued-vp` NO - capacity, and it is answerable today.** Rhonda's
MISSING is entirely about named people and honest limits. Her own CHANGE says an
honest "we would not shoot these ourselves" would move her more than a claim
that Fieldhouse would.

## What landed and what resisted

**Landed:** the sole-proof argument as a strategic idea; the packaging callout
system as a scalable mechanism; the specific process claims (twelve-hour cure,
hand-tied casing, small batches); the fact that the campaign uses what Marra
actually does rather than something invented.

**Resisted:** the film series as the opening and primary move (four seats, three
incompatible reasons); the commercial structure (two seats); the absence of
named people (one seat); the absence of any acknowledgment that an internal team
exists (one seat); the comparative claim (specialist gate).

## What was missing

Named across the run and not present in the artifact: a deliverable schedule; a
named team with allocations; a change-order trigger and notice period; a fixed
or explained film fee; a phased structure with a review gate; a stated leading
indicator; an asset matrix; the small-format packaging execution; the internal
working process and facility-access ask; substantiation for the comparative
claim.

Every one of those is answerable by Fieldhouse this week. None requires changing
the idea.

## Evidence

The strongest evidence in this run is convergent praise for the idea from seats
with nothing in common - a founder who wants it braver, a brand manager who
wants it safer, and an in-house creative lead who is structurally motivated to
reject it all pointed at the same concept. That is not a pattern a weak idea
produces.

The second-strongest is that the four most-cited fixes all attach to the
container, not the contents: sequence the callouts first, fix the commercial
document, name the team, and reframe one claim.

## Dissent

`legal-reviewer` FLAG on the central claim. `procurement-lead` NO on an
unscoreable proposal. `builds-it-in-house` NO on scope that displaces rather
than complements a real internal team. `agency-fatigued-vp` NO on unnamed
capacity. `finance-partner` NO that is really a phased yes.

None of the five is an objection to the work.

## Limitations

These are composites, not Marra's committee. Fieldhouse has met two of these
eleven people. `builds-it-in-house` was edited to a two-person internal team,
which makes her a materially softer hard-no than the shipped version, and that
edit reduces how much her NO tells us. Nine seats run on shipped assumptions.
No calibration data exists yet for this roster - this panel has never been
checked against a real Marra decision.
