## Agreement clusters

**The strategic argument is right, and even the seats saying no said so.** Cited
by `cmo-defender` (LANDS: "it is true and I have never heard it said that
cleanly"), `agency-fatigued-vp` (LANDS: "nobody has said that to us out loud"),
and - most tellingly - the hard-no `ops-vp` (LANDS: "the observation that we will
not beat the nationals on logistics is correct and I have said it myself for
years").

When a seat built to reject you endorses your strategic premise, the premise is
not the problem.

**The commercial structure is the strongest part of the pitch.** `procurement-lead`
(LANDS: "'all-in, no separate production' removes the entire category of dispute
I usually deal with"), `agency-fatigued-vp` (LANDS: clean exit),
`finance-partner` (LANDS: fixed fee and six-month stop). Three seats that
normally generate the objections had almost none. This is a rare cluster and
Nine Arrows should know their proposal document is genuinely good.

**Nothing in the campaign is measurable, and four seats said it in four
languages.** `cmo-defender` MISSING (a leading indicator someone already
tracks), `performance-lead` RESISTS ("four executions, zero measurable
channels"), `finance-partner` MISSING (the month-three indicator), `ops-vp`
REACTION ("nobody able to tell me what changed"). Four seats, one hole.

## Consequential dissent

**`cmo-defender` NO is the most consequential verdict in this run.** Dana is the
seat that has to carry the recommendation upward, she believes the idea, and she
said she cannot sell it - LANDS and RESISTS in direct opposition, which is the
signature failure this seat exists to detect. Her CHANGE names the fix precisely:
tie the expert directory to quote requests, and she has a number and a pitch.

A pitch that convinces the CMO and cannot arm her is a pitch that dies quietly
after a good meeting.

**`ops-vp` NO at intensity 5 - and read the second half of it.** The
studio-authored hard-no objected on two grounds. The first is expected: money
belongs in inventory and drivers. The second is not, and it is the sharpest thing
in the run - **the concept uses his 340 people as a marketing asset and says
nothing about what they get from it.** His CHANGE: "show me it makes my counter
staff's job better, not just more visible. If the directory means fewer
wrong-part calls reaching my branches, I would listen."

That is a hard-no naming his own conversion terms, and the terms are cheap. The
campaign is built on Verrick's people; a version that also makes those people's
jobs better is the same campaign with one more slide, and it converts the seat
most likely to kill it.

**`legal-reviewer` FLAG - a process gap, not a claim problem.** Alicia cleared
the advertising claims entirely (unusual) and flagged the publication of 340
named individuals with no consent mechanism, no declination path, and no plan
for departures - with the print directory as the sharpest case. Her CHANGE says
it plainly: a process problem with a known solution, not a reason to abandon the
idea.

**`performance-lead` NO at intensity 4, and his CHANGE is the cheapest fix in
the run.** Make "ask the person who knows" a searchable web resource with the
questions as pages. That single change gives the campaign a measurable channel,
a compounding asset, and answers `cmo-defender`'s MISSING and
`finance-partner`'s MISSING at the same time.

**`finance-partner` NO is a phased yes.** "Name one thing we already track that
should move, and by when. I would approve it that day."

  `internal-champion` - **who also has no authority** - while every seat that can

  said yes to the *paperwork* rather than the campaign (`procurement-lead`, who
  stated outright: "I have no view on whether the idea is any good").

  **Read that pattern precisely.** The two most enthusiastic seats in this run
  are the two with the least power. The seats with power are neutral-on-terms or
  negative-on-substance. This is the wrong-room signature, and it is the reason
  this run resolves to STOP rather than REVISE.

  Compare with example 01, where Hollis was equally enthusiastic and the alarm
  did NOT trip, because core buyers matched his intensity at higher confidence.
  The seat's own reaction is not the signal. Its position relative to the buyers
  is.

- **Wrong-fit more positive than core?** Tied with one no-authority core seat and
  above every seat with authority. Yes, effectively.
- **Near-identical reactions?** No. Ten distinct positions.
- **Generic consultant language?** No.
- **Degraded execution?** No.

**Integrity check: ONE WARNING TRIPPED.** The enthusiasm in this run is
concentrated in seats that cannot buy.

## Evidence

The strongest evidence in this run is that the strategic premise survived
contact with a seat built to reject it. `ops-vp` endorsed the argument and
rejected the spend, which separates the idea from its packaging as cleanly as
this panel ever manages.

The second-strongest is the four-seat convergence on unmeasurability, expressed
in four different vocabularies by four people with different jobs.

## Dissent

`cmo-defender` NO - believes it and cannot sell it upward. `ops-vp` NO at
intensity 5 - money belongs in service, and the people carrying the campaign get
nothing from it. `performance-lead` NO - no measurable channel exists.
`finance-partner` NO - no criteria attached to the decision point.
`legal-reviewer` FLAG - 340 named individuals, no consent mechanism.

## Limitations

Composites, not Verrick's committee. Nine of ten seats run on shipped
assumptions. The `ops-vp` hard-no is the studio's own construction, which makes
it the most speculative seat in the run and also the one that produced the
sharpest finding - treat that finding as a hypothesis to test, not as
established. No calibration data exists for this roster.

## Next step

```text

```

**STOP, not REVISE - and this is the distinction worth understanding.** STOP here
does not mean the idea is bad. Every seat that engaged with the argument found it
correct, including the hard-no. STOP means the pitch as constructed is aimed at
the wrong room: it is built to be loved by people who cannot authorize it, and it
gives the people who can authorize it nothing to act on.

A rewrite would produce three better-worded versions of a pitch with the same
structural problem. The foundation has to change first.

**What has to change before this is worth re-running:**

1. **Give it a commercial mechanism.** `cmo-defender` CHANGE and
   `performance-lead` CHANGE point at the same fix from opposite ends: make
   "ask the person who knows" a searchable web resource, and route the expert
   directory into quote requests. One change, four objections answered.
2. **Name one existing metric and a month-three checkpoint.**
   `finance-partner` said he would approve that day.
3. **Answer the `ops-vp` question, which is the real one.** The campaign is
   built on 340 people and says nothing about what those people get. A version
   where the expert directory routes the right question to the right person -
   fewer wrong-part calls reaching the branches, less time on calls that were
   never theirs - makes the counter staff's job better, not merely more visible.
   That is the same campaign, and it converts the seat most likely to kill it.
4. **Add the consent, declination, update, and departure mechanism**
   (`legal-reviewer` CHANGE). Process, not concept.

**The smallest real-world step that could prove this panel wrong:** ask Verrick's
CMO directly what she would need to take this to her president. `cmo-defender`
is the load-bearing NO in this run, the panel says she believes the idea and
cannot carry it, and that is one question on the next call.

Then re-run. This is a strong idea with a missing spine, not a weak idea.
