# What to notice in this example

## 1. The two examples have almost the same score and completely different results

Example 01 scored 57.0 and resolved REVISE. This one scored 55.35 and resolved
STOP. Two points apart, opposite instructions.

**That is the entire argument for why the score is not the finding.** The
difference is not in the number, it is in WHO said yes. In example 01 the
enthusiasm was spread across seats with authority. Here it is concentrated in the
two seats that cannot authorize anything.

If you take one thing from these examples: read the decision table by authority,
not by count.

## 2. The wrong-fit alarm, shown firing

`budget-was-never-real` returned an identical reaction shape in both examples -
intensity 5, confidence LOW, nothing resisting, nothing missing. In example 01
that did not trip the alarm. Here it did.

The seat's own answer is not the signal. Its position relative to the buyers is.
This is the most expensive pattern in the pitching business - a wonderful meeting
with the people who love you and cannot buy - and it is very hard to see from
inside the room, because the room feels great.

## 3. A hard-no produced the best finding in the run

`ops-vp` was written by the studio during setup, because the shipped
`builds-it-in-house` seat did not exist at this client and **the standard does
not allow dropping the dissent** - it has to be replaced.

That replacement seat produced the sharpest objection anyone raised: the campaign
is built on 340 employees and never says what those employees get out of it. Not
a marketing objection. A human one, from the person responsible for them.

And it came with its own conversion terms, which are cheap: make the directory
route the right question to the right person, so the counter staff field fewer
calls that were never theirs. Same campaign, one more slide, and the person most
likely to kill it becomes the person defending it.

**Notice what a studio that skipped the replacement dissent seat would have
gotten:** a 5-3 panel with no hard-no, reading much more positive, and no
knowledge of the objection that would have ended the pitch.

## 4. STOP does not mean the idea is bad

Every seat that engaged with the strategic argument said it was right, including
the hard-no. STOP was returned because the pitch was structurally aimed at the
wrong room, and rewriting the words would not move that.

REVISE means answer the objections. STOP means change something underneath them
first. Getting that distinction wrong costs a studio three polished versions of
the same losing pitch.

## 5. The proposal document was the best part, and the panel said so

Three seats that normally generate objections - procurement, finance, the
fatigued VP - had almost none. `procurement-lead` called it the cleanest
creative-services proposal he had seen.

That is worth as much as a criticism. Nine Arrows now knows their commercial
document is a genuine asset and should stay exactly as it is while everything
else changes.

## 6. The gate record exists even though nothing was refused

`2-refusal-or-gate.md` records a PASS. A studio should be able to see the check
ran rather than assume it. A gate that only leaves a trace when it fires is a
gate you cannot audit.

Note also what the AI did there: it flagged the consent question BEFORE running,
as a heads-up rather than a refusal, so the studio would recognize the legal
reaction as the panel working rather than as the panel misreading the idea.
