# Evidence Notes

What this panel is actually based on. Keep it honest - this file is what stops a
useful thinking tool from being mistaken for client research.

## Status of the shipped roster

**The thirteen seats that ship with this kit are composites built from common
client-side decision-committee patterns. None of them is based on a real named
person, and none is based on your client.**

That makes the shipped panel a good structured thinking instrument and weak
evidence. It becomes real evidence only as you replace shipped assumptions with
things you actually know.

## What we do not know that would change a verdict

The most useful section in this file. List the questions whose answers would move
a seat, and which you could just ask.

- Is there an incumbent agency, and is this review genuine or a benchmark?
- Is the budget approved, or is it being sought?
- Is there an in-house creative team, and how big?
- Who actually signs?
- Has legal been involved before or will they see it at the end?

Every one of those is answerable with a direct question, and a direct answer beats
any panel run. This kit is for the objections you cannot ask about.

## Calibration ledger

After the real pitch, record what actually happened. This is the only thing that
makes the panel better over time, and it is the step everyone skips.

| Pitch | Panel said | Real outcome | Objections the panel missed | Objections the panel invented |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| | | | | |

**How to use it:** do not tune a weight after one surprising result. Look for the
same miss across three or more comparable runs. A seat that repeatedly invents
objections nobody raised should be narrowed. A seat that repeatedly misses the
objection that actually killed the pitch should be rewritten from what you now
know.

Until this ledger has entries, the panel has earned no accuracy claim of any
kind. Do not make one.
