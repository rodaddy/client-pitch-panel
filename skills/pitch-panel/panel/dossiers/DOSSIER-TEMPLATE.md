## Panel role

Why this seat exists, which decision difference it represents, and what healthy
agreement and healthy dissent each look like from this person. For a `hard-no` or
`wrong-fit` seat, say plainly that conversion is a warning signal and what it
would mean.

- Evidence: <a second, distinct basis> - <a different constraint or pattern>

If the studio has met the real person, say so here and mark it as first-hand
observation. That is the strongest evidence this kit can carry.

## Demographics and context

Only traits that materially change the decision. Do not invent precise age,
income, ethnicity, disability, or legal status to make the person feel real.

## Professional or life background

Relevant experience, environment, responsibilities, and constraints. What they
have lived through that shapes how they read a pitch.

## Primary goals and motivations

What this person is actually trying to accomplish - in their job and in their
career. Not what the studio wishes they wanted.

## Secondary goals

Real but non-primary motivations. Often where a pitch finds an unexpected way in.

## Pain points

The practical friction, cost, risk, or unmet need. Be specific about the
mechanism, not the feeling.

## Emotional frustrations

What creates anxiety, resentment, fatigue, embarrassment, or indifference.
Without sensationalizing.

## Channels and content habits

How this person actually encounters the pitch - live in the room, as a forwarded
PDF, as a summary from someone else. Which pages they read first, which they
skip, and what they never see at all. This is frequently the most decision-
relevant section and the most often skipped.

## Decision-making process

Authority (vote, gate, influence, none), proof threshold, timing, risk tolerance,
what would actually move them, and how fast they decide. Name the mechanism by
which they affect the outcome.

## Buying or action history

Prior agency relationships, prior awards and rejections, what they learned, what
they now do differently. The pattern matters more than the anecdotes.

## Voice notes

Natural phrases this person would actually use. Specific but not caricatured.
Include what they say when they like something and what they say when they do
not - those are often confusingly similar, and that is worth capturing.

## Boundaries and likely no

What this person will reject, ignore, distrust, or never do. For `wrong-fit` and
`hard-no` seats, state explicitly what a healthy no looks like and what would
have to be true for them to move.
